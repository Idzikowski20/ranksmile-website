A prompt is a question you want to know the answer to, in the sense that a buyer would ask it. Ranksmile puts each one to every engine you track and records what came back.

Picking them well is most of the work. It is also where the common mistake lives.

## Do not track your brand name

Ask an engine about your company and it will describe your company. You learn that it has heard of you, which you already knew, and nothing about whether you are winning.

Track the questions asked _before_ anyone knows your name. "Best rank tracker for agencies" tells you who owns the category. "Ranksmile pricing" tells you nothing.

## What a useful prompt looks like

<DefinitionList>

Buying intent
: The question someone asks when they are choosing, not when they are researching idly

Category-level
: Names the problem or the category, not a vendor

Natural
: Phrased the way a person types it into an assistant, in full sentences if that is how they would ask

Specific enough to have an answer
: "Best CRM" is too broad to produce a stable answer. "Best CRM for a two-person agency" is not.

</DefinitionList>

## A good starting set

Ten questions, marked core, covering:

- The category, asked plainly ("what is the best tool for X")
- The category with your qualifier ("...for agencies", "...for small teams", "...in Poland")
- The comparison people actually make ("X vs Y")
- The problem before the category ("how do I stop losing traffic to AI answers")
- The objection ("is X worth the money")

That is enough to see shape within a week. Add the long tail once you can tell which topics you are absent from.

## Priorities

Each prompt carries a priority, and the priority sets how often it re-scans: core daily, supporting weekly, long-tail every two weeks.

Be disciplined about core. The temptation is to mark everything core because everything feels important, and the result is an allowance spent evenly across questions that matter unequally. Ten core prompts and forty supporting ones is a healthier shape than fifty core.

## How many

Fifty per Brand Space. That is more than most teams need and considerably more than most teams can act on.

<Admonition type="tip" title="The test for whether a prompt earns its place">
If the answer changed tomorrow, would you do anything differently? If not, it is a vanity prompt. Track it as long-tail or not at all.
</Admonition>

## Reading the results

Each prompt comes back with whether you were mentioned, whether you were cited, and who else was. Three of those are about you and one is about the market, and the market column is often the one worth reading first: it tells you which competitors the engines treat as the default answer.

[Competitors](/docs/ai-visibility/competitors) aggregates that across every prompt you track. [Sources](/docs/ai-visibility/sources) does the same for the pages being cited.
