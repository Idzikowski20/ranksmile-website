Competitors aggregates the brand names that appeared across every answer your tracked prompts returned, and shows how often each one came up.

It answers a question that is hard to ask any other way: in the eyes of the engines, who is the default answer in this market?

## Why the list surprises people

The brands the engines name are frequently not the brands you consider your competitors.

Two reasons. Engines answer the question asked, not the question you wish had been asked, so a broad prompt pulls in adjacent categories. And engines favour whoever has been written about most clearly, which rewards companies with strong content over companies with strong products.

Both are worth knowing. The first tells you your prompt was too broad. The second tells you where the gap actually is.

## Reading it per prompt

The aggregate is the headline, but the per-prompt breakdown is where the work is. A competitor who appears in every answer is a market leader and not much of an insight. A competitor who owns exactly three of your prompts has done something specific on those three topics, and that is a thing you can go and look at.

Open [Sources](/docs/ai-visibility/sources) for those prompts and read the pages being cited.

## Turning it into keywords

A competitor who dominates a topic in AI answers usually also ranks for it in search. [Keyword research](/docs/search/keyword-research) has a gap report that takes a competitor domain and returns the terms they rank for and you do not.

Running that against the name at the top of this list is the most direct path from "they get cited and we do not" to a list of pages to write.

<Admonition type="tip" title="Track the ones that keep appearing">
If a brand you had not considered a competitor shows up in a quarter of your answers, that is the market telling you something. Add them to the gap analysis before you dismiss it.
</Admonition>

## What it will not tell you

It does not tell you why a competitor is favoured. The answer is almost always in their content, and reading it is not something this module can do for you.

It also does not measure share of voice in any rigorous sense. Generated answers vary between runs, so treat the counts as a ranking rather than a metric. A competitor at 40% and one at 35% are not meaningfully different. One at 40% and one at 5% are.
