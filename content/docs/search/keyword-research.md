Two ways in. Start from a word and expand, or start from a competitor and subtract.

The second is faster and more honest, and it is the one most people skip.

## The competitor gap

Give it a competitor's domain. It returns the terms they rank for that you do not, with the volume and difficulty behind each.

This is the shortest path from "we should do more SEO" to a list of specific pages to write, because the list is not hypothetical. Somebody is already ranking for those terms, which proves the demand exists and that the topic is winnable by a site in your market.

It also tends to contradict at least one thing the team believed. That is the point of running it.

<Callout title="Pick the right competitor">
Not the biggest company in your market. The one you lose deals to, or the one that keeps appearing in [AI visibility competitors](/docs/ai-visibility/competitors). A gap report against a company ten times your size returns terms you cannot win.
</Callout>

## Seed expansion

Give it a term and it returns related ones: variations, questions, longer phrases around the same intent.

Useful when you are opening a topic you have no presence in, or checking whether a topic is deeper than the one obvious term. Less useful than the gap report when you already have competitors worth measuring against, because expansion tells you what exists while the gap tells you what is missing.

## Reading the output

Three columns decide whether a term is worth anything.

<DefinitionList>

Volume
: How many people search it. High volume with no commercial intent is a traffic number, not a revenue one.

Difficulty
: How hard the current results are to displace. A high-volume term you cannot rank for is worth less than a modest one you can.

Relevance
: Whether it is your business. No tool can judge this for you and it overrides the other two.

</DefinitionList>

The common mistake is optimizing for the first column. The terms worth having are usually mid-volume, mid-difficulty and unambiguously yours.

## Coverage gaps

Beyond individual terms, research surfaces topics your content does not answer at all. These pair with [fanout queries](/docs/ai-visibility/fanout-queries), which show the same thing from the assistants' side.

When both point at the same missing topic, that is the strongest brief the product produces.

## Where results go

Everything lands in the [keyword list](/docs/search/keyword-list), where you decide per term whether to track it, write for it, or discard it.
