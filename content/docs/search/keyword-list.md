The keyword list is where terms live before and after you decide what to do with them. Research puts things in it, tracking pulls things from it, and the middle is a queue.

## Why it is separate from tracking

Tracking answers "where am I". The list answers "what is there".

Keeping them apart means research can surface two hundred terms without flooding your position chart with two hundred lines, and you can sit with a term for a week before committing to track it.

## What ends up here

<DefinitionList>

Tracked terms
: What you are following positions for

Research results
: Everything [keyword research](/docs/search/keyword-research) surfaced from a seed or a competitor

Gaps
: Terms a competitor ranks for and you do not

Coverage gaps
: Questions your content does not answer, drawn from what the engines and the search results show people asking

</DefinitionList>

## Working through it

The useful discipline is to treat the list as a queue rather than an archive.

A term arrives from research. You decide one of three things: track it, write for it, or discard it. Terms with no decision attached accumulate until the list is unreadable, and an unreadable list gets ignored, which wastes the research that filled it.

<Callout title="Discarding is a decision">
Most research output should be discarded. A term with real volume that is nowhere near your business is not an opportunity, it is a distraction with a number attached. Getting comfortable saying no to it is what keeps the list useful.
</Callout>

## Turning terms into work

Terms you decide to act on go two ways.

Track it if you already have a page, so you can see whether the page is moving. Write for it if you do not, which means a brief, a draft, and a [content score](/docs/content/content-score) check before it goes out. [Automations](/docs/content/automations) can carry that from the list to a scheduled piece.

## Limits

How much research you can run per month depends on your plan, and that is the thing that fills this list. See [plans and limits](/docs/introduction/plans).
