Every module produces findings. The audit finds broken pages, research finds missing terms, AI visibility finds topics you are absent from, content score finds pages that are not competing.

Left separate, that is four lists and a decision about which to open. Recommendations merges them into one, ordered by likely impact.

## What it draws on

<DocsList title="Sources it weighs" theme="docs">
<a href="/docs/site/site-audit">Site audit findings, weighted by how many pages each affects</a>
<a href="/docs/search/performance">Performance data, so a problem on a page with traffic outranks the same problem on a page without</a>
<a href="/docs/ai-visibility/overview">AI visibility gaps, including topics where competitors are cited and you are not</a>
<a href="/docs/content/content-score">Content scores, so a page close to competing ranks above one that is far away</a>
</DocsList>

## Why the order is the product

A flat list of two hundred SEO findings is not actionable, it is demoralizing. Most of it does not matter, and the part that does is buried.

The ordering weighs two things together: how much a fix is likely to move, and how much traffic or visibility sits behind the page it affects. A missing meta description on a page nobody visits is real and irrelevant. The same issue on your third-highest-traffic page is worth an hour.

## Working through it

Take the top item. Do it. Let the next crawl and the next scans confirm it landed, then take the next one.

The temptation is to open the list, feel overwhelmed, and batch fifteen small fixes at once. That gets things done but destroys your ability to tell what worked, because everything changed in the same week.

<Callout title="One change, then measure">
This matters most for anything touching content or crawlability. Rankings and citations move on a delay of weeks. Fixing five things in one afternoon means five candidate explanations for whatever happens next month.
</Callout>

## When to ignore it

When you know something it does not.

The ordering is drawn from measurable signals, and it cannot know that a launch is in three weeks, or that a page is scheduled for deletion, or that a client has asked for something specific. Recommendations is a strong default, not an instruction.

## Where it connects

Items that mean "write or fix a page" hand off to [content score](/docs/content/content-score) for the brief and [Automations](/docs/content/automations) for the scheduling. Items that mean "fix something technical" are work on your site.
