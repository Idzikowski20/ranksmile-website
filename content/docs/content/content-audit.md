Content score examines one page. Content audit does it across the library, so you can see which pages are competing, which are close, and which are not in the race.

The value is in the sorting, not the scoring. Most sites have a long tail of pages nobody has looked at in two years, and the audit is what surfaces the handful of them that are worth an hour.

## The three groups worth finding

**Close to competing.** Pages scoring just below the threshold for their topic. These are the best use of your time in the whole product: the topic is right, the page exists, and the gap is usually three concepts and a restructured introduction.

**Cannibalizing each other.** Two or more pages aimed at the same query, splitting the signal between them. Neither ranks as well as one would. The fix is a merge and a redirect, and it is unglamorous work that moves rankings more reliably than new content does.

**Decayed.** Pages that used to compete and no longer do, because the results moved and they did not. They are easy to find here because the targets are drawn from the current results, so a page that has not changed while its competition has will show the gap.

## What to do with the rest

Most pages will score low and deserve to. Not every page is trying to rank, and a support article that does its job is not a failure because it does not compete for a commercial term.

Judge a page by whether it is trying to rank for something. If it is not, the score is not measuring anything you care about.

<Callout title="Deleting is a valid outcome">
A page that ranks for nothing, converts nobody and has not been updated in three years is costing you crawl budget and diluting your topical signal. Removing it, with a redirect if anything links to it, is a real improvement and the audit is how you find the candidates.
</Callout>

## A sensible cadence

Quarterly is enough for most sites. The targets move with the results, so running it weekly produces noise and a sense of obligation rather than insight.

Run it out of cycle after a significant change to your market, a competitor relaunch, or a visible shift in the results for your main terms.

## Where it connects

Individual pages open in [content score](/docs/content/content-score) for the full breakdown. Work that comes out of the audit can be scheduled through [Automations](/docs/content/automations), and [Recommendations](/docs/site/recommendations) folds the findings in with everything else.
