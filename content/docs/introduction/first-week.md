Some of what Ranksmile measures needs time to become meaningful. A single AI visibility scan is a sample, not a measurement, and a position with no history behind it tells you nothing about direction. So the order matters more than the speed.

This is the sequence that leaves you with something worth reading at the end of the week, rather than five modules all started on day six.

<Steps>

## Create the space and connect Search Console

[Create a Brand Space](/docs/introduction/brand-spaces) for the domain, then connect its Search Console property.

Do this first, before anything else. Performance data has history behind it, and the sooner it is pulled in, the more context every other number has.

## Run a site audit

Start the [crawl](/docs/site/site-audit). It takes a while on a large site, so start it and go do the next step.

When it finishes, open [AI Search Health](/docs/site/ai-search-health) before you look at anything else in the result. If an answer engine's crawler is blocked, that single fact explains more about your AI visibility than the rest of the week's data will.

## Set up ten AI prompts, marked core

This is the step people leave until Thursday and then cannot read.

Write ten questions a buyer would ask before they know your name, and mark them core so they re-scan daily. By day seven you have a week of trend. Start them on day four and you have three points, which is not enough to tell a change from noise.

Skip your brand name. An engine will mention you when asked about you, and that teaches you nothing.

See [choosing prompts](/docs/ai-visibility/prompts) for what separates a useful prompt from a flattering one.

## Track your real keywords

Add the terms you actually compete for to [keyword tracking](/docs/search/keyword-tracking). Positions refresh daily, so the same logic applies: earlier means more trend by the end of the week.

## Run keyword research against one competitor

Pick the competitor you lose to most often and run the [gap report](/docs/search/keyword-research). It is the fastest honest answer to what you are missing, and it usually contradicts at least one thing everybody on the team believed.

## Score your three most important pages

Run [content score](/docs/content/content-score) on the three pages that matter commercially. The breakdown tells you why they are or are not competing, term by term.

## Read Recommendations

By now [Recommendations](/docs/site/recommendations) has drawn on all of it. Open it last, not first: it is most useful once the audit, the keywords and the first scans have given it something to rank.

</Steps>

<Admonition type="note" title="On the trial">
The Growth trial runs seven days, which is exactly the length of this list. That is deliberate, and it is why steps one to three belong on day one. See [plans and limits](/docs/introduction/plans).
</Admonition>

## What a good first week looks like

You should end it able to answer four questions: whether anything technical is blocking you, which competitor owns the terms you want, whether assistants mention you for your core topics, and which of your pages are not competing and why.

If you can answer those, the tool is set up. Everything after that is doing the work it found.
