They answer two different questions, and you need both.

**Rank tracking** asks: for this keyword, which position does my page hold? One keyword, one number, one direction to move it. This is the measurement SEO has run on for twenty years and it still works, because people still click results.

**AI visibility** asks: when someone puts this question to an assistant, does the answer mention my brand, and does it cite my domain? There's no position to hold. You're either in the answer or you're not.

## Why a good ranking no longer settles it

A generated answer sits above the results and often ends the session. The user reads it and leaves. Your first-position page was on screen and never got the click.

Worse, the answer can be built partly from your content while crediting someone else. That's the gap AI visibility exists to catch: you did the work that informed the answer, and a competitor got named in it.

## Reading them together

The interesting cases are where the two disagree.

- **Ranking well, invisible in AI.** Your page is technically fine but reads like a brochure. Assistants quote pages that answer a question directly. Check whether your top page actually answers the question in its first paragraph.
- **Cited in AI, ranking poorly.** The content is good and the domain is weak. That's a link and authority problem, not a writing one.
- **Falling in both at once.** Check Site Audit first. This pattern usually means something technical broke, not that your content aged.

## Where each one lives

Rank tracking is under **Keyword tracking** in a Brand Space, with the keyword list and research tools beside it. AI visibility has its own section: Overview, Sources, Competitors, Prompts and Fanout Queries.

Sources is the one to open first. It shows which of your pages are actually being cited, which is usually not the page you'd have guessed.
